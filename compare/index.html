    Sources & limitations

    Green: warmer water, greater transparency, weaker currents or lower waves when both range endpoints favor one destination. Mixed ranges, ties and unavailable data stay neutral. Coverage varies; this is not a safety rating.

    Ranges are the minimum and maximum of valid site samples in the selected month, not area averages, seasonal extremes or destination rankings.

    How coverage is calculated

    All public records in the selected destinations are queried, including small destinations and records without generated pages. Same-named places in different countries remain separate; physical site aliases are not merged.

    Coverage counts site queries, not independent observations or statistical confidence. Sites may share grid cells. Missing data and failed requests remain separate and are never replaced with zero.

    Environmental data and source periods

    • WOA23 · Water temperature: 1991–2020 · 0.25° · 5 m
    • Copernicus · Water clarity: 2016–2025 · 4 km. Secchi transparency: higher values mean clearer water. This is not measured horizontal dive visibility.
    • Copernicus · Current: 1993–2016 · 10 m (~9.57 m) · ~37 km. Modelled monthly current at about 10 m depth. This is the speed of the monthly average current vector, not the maximum or tidal current.
    • Copernicus · Wave height: 1993–2020 · 0.2°. Significant wave height is the average height of the highest third of waves. Individual waves can be higher.

    Temperature and transparency may use valid cells within 25 km. Currents and waves retain the nearest shipped-cell mask; no nearby replacement is applied.

    Generated using E.U. Copernicus Marine Service Information. DOI: 10.48670/moi-00021; 10.48670/moi-00022.

    Why current coverage can be limited

    Currents retain every fourth native grid point (~37 km). Masked cells limit Komodo coverage. Missing data does not mean no current or prove finer-source absence; monthly speeds do not establish dive safety.

    Bali / Komodo guidance and local conditions

    PADI guidance: Bali April–October dry season; Komodo April–November generally drier, especially central/north. Guidance is separate from climatology. Confirm conditions and suitability with a local dive professional.
